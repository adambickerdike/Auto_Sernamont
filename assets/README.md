# Assets

Every figure in the documentation is either a diagram of the real instrument or
a computed figure generated by a script in [`../tools/`](../tools/). Nothing is
a stock illustration, and nothing is drawn by eye.

## Source diagrams

| File | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `setup_schematic.png` | The optical and electrical schematic of the bench: the beam path from the 1550 nm laser down through the chip to the detector, and the electrical path through the bias tee and switching matrix. |
| `chip_gds_layout.png` | The chip GDS layout: 100 coplanar electrode pairs on a 2.5 mm pitch, fanned out to bond pads on all four edges. |

Shared style and physics helpers live in `tools/_figstyle.py` and
`tools/_polarisation.py`.

> **Note**
> The crystallographic drawings use atom radii chosen for legibility, and the
> phase-diagram boundaries are schematic. The displacements, axes, phase
> sequence and free-energy landscapes follow the accepted physics rather than
> being invented, and the pages that use them say where a boundary position is
> illustrative rather than computed.

## Curated measurement data

`data/` holds eight small `dc_hysteresis.csv` files copied from real runs,
together with `manifest.json` recording which run and pixel each came from.
They are committed deliberately, and they are the only measurement data in the
repository, so that the two measured-data figures can be regenerated from a
fresh clone with no access to the lab file server.

> **Note**
> Two of those runs predate the ±40 V production ceiling and were acquired on a
> ±50 V trajectory. The figures say so where it matters.

## Brand

`logo/banner.png`, `logo/logo.png` and `logo/logo-light.png` are generated by
`tools/fig_brand.py`. The mark is the measurement itself: a beam passing
through a polarisation ellipse onto an electrode array, with one pixel lit.
